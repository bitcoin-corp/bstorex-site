How it works

You pay after the proof, not before.

This is how bstore, the first network we plan to list, works today in its own software. bStoreX will be the front door: one place to find providers, compare their terms and pay from your wallet.

  1. 01

    Put it once

    You publish a file once and name a bounty for holding it. The providers serving your bucket receive it, and each decides whether to hold it.

  2. 02

    Providers prove they hold it

    A provider that holds the file says so on the BSV blockchain, then has to pass a challenge: your own client samples small pieces of the file and checks each one against the file's proof root.

  3. 03

    You pay after the proof

    The first providers to pass are paid by your own transaction, split by rank. You choose how many providers you pay. Nothing is paid for a promise.

  4. 04

    Your S3 tools work

    bstore's gateway speaks S3, so the AWS CLI, the SDKs and most S3 clients need only an endpoint and keys.

When you read a file back, every piece is checked against its hash, and the file is written whole or not at all. Anyone can buy a fresh proof, or more time for a file, whenever they like.

The limits, in bstore's own words: availability is a market outcome, and enforcement is reputation in this version. A challenge checks a sample, not every byte, so a larger sample is more likely to catch missing data. Read bstore's limits

Networks

The first network: bstore.

Each network on bStoreX will have its own providers, terms and way of proving what it holds. We start with one, and plan to add more.

bstore

ExperimentalNot listed yet
“Object storage as a market on Bitcoin: providers race for a bounty to hold an object, and readers prove every byte, with an S3 gateway.”

The project's own description

  • ProofSampled challenges, checked by the writer's own client
  • PaymentFrom the writer's own transaction, after a passed challenge
  • AccessIts own command line, or S3 through its gateway
  • ProvidersNone public yet, so none listed here
